LinkedIn for Students Concept

Over three months, four college students researched, designed, and validated a comprehensive student-centric experience on LinkedIn — pitching directly to 16 LinkedIn employees and LinkedIn's Chief Product Officer.

Team Sarah Ahmed, Milap Patel, Khushi Shelat, Shobha Dasari
Sarah's Role Product Designer (UI/UX, Figma Prototypes, Design System)
Validation Scope 50+ User Interviews, ~200 Surveyed Students, 16 Pitches
Outcome Executive Review with CPO Tomer Cohen & Product Team

Project Context: Bringing the Team Together

In late 2020, Milap Patel (UC Davis '22) published an initial case study exploring "LinkedIn Coffee Chats." Around the same time, Khushi Shelat (Penn '23) and Shobha Dasari (Stanford '23) were exploring student career friction at the Product Buds Protothon. Recognizing a shared ambition to transform the early-career experience, they joined forces.

After the first week of initial discovery, the team recognized the need for an end-to-end product designer to translate complex research insights into high-fidelity prototypes that conformed strictly to LinkedIn's existing enterprise UI patterns—and that is where Sarah Ahmed (Santa Clara '21) stepped in as Lead Product Designer.

Over a rigorous three-month sprint, our virtual team interviewed 50+ students, surveyed nearly 200 participants, designed 3 iterative prototype suites, conducted bottoms-up market sizing, and pitched to 16 LinkedIn employees across Product, Design, and Business Development.

Understanding the Problem: The Four Student Pain Points

LinkedIn’s demographic data reveals that 20.4% of its active user base is between 18 and 24 years old, representing more than 46 million students and recent graduates. Yet, the platform's core information architecture remains optimized for established mid-to-senior professionals with decade-long resumes.

Through our preliminary interviews and subsequent survey of ~200 students, four distinct structural pain points emerged:

91% Dissatisfaction

1. Pricing & Value Gap

91% of surveyed students stated that LinkedIn Premium ($29.99+/mo) was not worth the cost. Standard Premium features (e.g., InMail credits, competitive salary insights) offer negligible value to students without established work histories.

"I'd really like if LinkedIn discounted Premium for students and new grads who are searching for their first full-time job."
66% Cold Drop-off

2. Mentorship & Outreach Anxiety

On average, 66% of cold outreach messages sent by students lead nowhere. Students reported feeling significant anxiety about cold messaging senior professionals, fearing they would appear transactional or bothersome.

"Sending out 50 cold messages with 2 responses shouldn't be the name of the game."
36% Dislike Rate

3. Feed Toxicity & Culture

36% of students identified the main feed as their least favorite LinkedIn feature. The prevailing culture of performative "humblebrags" and motivational corporate essays triggers acute imposter syndrome among job seekers.

"I find the motivational posts on the feed to be fake, cringe-y, and not helpful at all (so I rarely read them)."
90% Demand

4. The Entry-Level Job Paradox

90% of surveyed students requested dedicated student support in job hunting. Most listings labeled "entry-level" paradoxically demand 2 to 4 years of prior experience, discouraging qualified applicants.

"It would be helpful if the Jobs side of LinkedIn was structured to better cater towards students."

Developing the Solution: Three Core Pillars

To directly address these pain points, we developed three deeply integrated feature sets within the LinkedIn ecosystem:

Pillar 01

Mentorship & Structured Coffee Chats

Rather than leaving mentorship to haphazard cold messaging, we created an intentional, low-friction pathway for student-professional networking:

  • "Open to Mentoring" Status: Professionals can toggle an opt-in badge indicating their willingness to give advice, specify their focus areas (e.g., resume review, portfolio critique, career transition), and cap monthly slots.
  • 1-Click Coffee Chat Scheduler: Native calendar integration allowing students to schedule 15-to-30 minute virtual sessions without exchanging emails or third-party links.
  • Mutual Agenda Templates: Standardized prompt chips and pre-meeting agendas ensure both the student and mentor have clear expectations, maximizing the value of each call.
Pillar 02

Student Hub & Smart Content Filtration

We reimagined the feed to deliver peer-to-peer solidarity, actionable career advice, and academic relevance:

  • Dedicated Student Hub: A focused community space featuring hackathon announcements, scholarship opportunities, club collaborations, and peer-to-peer Q&A.
  • Granular Feed Filters: Users can mute corporate flex posts, boastful status updates, or specific keywords while prioritizing industry insights, entry-level recruiting tips, and university alumni updates.
Pillar 03

Early-Career Job Tracker & Skill Bridge

Bridging the gap between academic coursework and real-world hiring prerequisites:

  • Embedded Application Tracker: A Kanban-style dashboard within LinkedIn Jobs enabling students to monitor application stages, follow-up deadlines, and recruiter responses.
  • Percentage Match Score: Algorithms analyze student project portfolios and coursework to generate an accurate readiness score.
  • LinkedIn Learning Integration: Direct skill-gap bridges on job postings. If an internship requires SQL, LinkedIn automatically links a 20-minute targeted course module to help the student achieve a 100% match.

Testing & Iteration: 50+ Think-Aloud Sessions

To validate our designs, Sarah built interactive prototypes in Figma, and the team conducted 20-minute Zoom usability tests with over 50 students from diverse academic institutions.

Participants shared their screens and used a "think-aloud" protocol while navigating the Mentorship, Feed, and Jobs prototypes. We synthesized the sessions using a strict color-coded quadrant matrix:

🟢 What Worked (Validated Strengths)

Students loved the intuitive coffee chat scheduling and expressed high enthusiasm for the embedded job application tracker, noting it would eliminate disparate spreadsheets.

🔴 What Failed (Constructive Critiques)

Initial mentorship prototypes lacked time boundaries; students worried mentors would feel overwhelmed. We introduced strict 15/30-minute time caps and monthly session limits.

🟠 UI & Interactivity Friction

Certain filter dropdowns inside the Student Hub were buried. Sarah restructured the navigation bar to sit prominently below the global search bar, matching LinkedIn's web hierarchy.

🟡 Opportunities & New Ideas

Students requested post-meeting note templates and icebreaker prompts. We added automated follow-up thank-you templates to foster lasting relationships.

Go-To-Market Strategy & Business Case

A great product design requires an equally strong business rationale. For a mature enterprise platform like LinkedIn, feature proposals must support company-level OKRs and revenue models.

Bottoms-Up Market Sizing: $1.1B TAM

We performed a bottoms-up market analysis to quantify the revenue potential of student adoption:

184M (18–24 Year-Olds on LinkedIn) × 60% (Willingness to Pay) × $10/year = $1.1 Billion Annual Market Opportunity

The 18–24 demographic on LinkedIn grew 30% faster than the platform's overall user base over the preceding four-year period, representing the single most critical pipeline for long-term subscriber lifetime value (LTV).

B2B2C University Licensing Model

Rather than placing financial strain on individual students, we proposed partnering directly with university career centers. Universities pay an annual tier-based institutional fee (benchmarked against Handshake and Symplicity) to grant students complimentary access to LinkedIn for Students.

Competitive Moat Analysis

We examined niche competitors across each pillar: Ladder (community feed), Mentor Mesh (mentorship), and Handshake (campus recruiting). LinkedIn's insurmountable advantage remains its global directory of verified alumni, corporate decision-makers, and hiring managers.

Pitching to LinkedIn & Meeting the CPO

Early on, we resolved not to design in a vacuum. We initiated cold outreach to LinkedIn professionals across Product Management, UX Design, Corporate Strategy, and Engineering. Over two weeks, we conducted eight 30-minute pitch sessions with 16 LinkedIn employees, iterating on our slide deck after every meeting based on internal feedback.

"By late February, after sharing our Whitepaper and 2-minute pitch video on LinkedIn, the post gained viral traction across the product community—and caught the attention of Tomer Cohen, LinkedIn's Chief Product Officer."

The Executive Review with Tomer Cohen (CPO)

Tomer Cohen commented on the post and requested a formal meeting. In March, our team attended an exhilarating 25-minute executive pitch session with Tomer. We prepared a concise one-pager explicitly mapping our proposed features to LinkedIn's overarching mission: "Connect the world's professionals to make them more productive and successful."

Tomer expressed deep interest in our data on the 18–24 cohort and the psychological nuances of student networking. Following the presentation, he connected our team with Liam Walsh, Head of Product Recruiting at LinkedIn, who coordinated individual coffee chats and mentorship sessions for each of us.

Reflections & Product Takeaways

1. Design Within Enterprise Design Systems

Designing for a platform with hundreds of millions of users taught me the balance between innovation and pattern consistency. By adopting LinkedIn's established typography, spacing, and icon grid, the new features felt native rather than foreign.

2. Microcopy and Psychological Safety

For early-career job seekers, UI friction is often emotional rather than technical. Thoughtful content design—such as pre-composed conversation starters and transparent status badges—transformed an intimidating process into an inviting experience.

3. Product Design Is Business Strategy

A compelling interface is only half the battle. Backing our designs with bottoms-up market sizing ($1.1B TAM) and an enterprise B2B2C licensing model was what earned us credibility in the executive room.
